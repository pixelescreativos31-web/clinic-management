# Especialidades médicas y adopción EMR (2024–2026)

> Scope note: This research targets independent doctors and small/mid clinics. US CDC/ONC data is the most rigorous source for adoption-by-specialty but its specialty breakdowns are coarse and the most-cited specialty percentages circulating online are from the **2015** NEHRS, not recent. Most specialty-level market sizing comes from commercial research firms (Grand View, Mordor, Towards Healthcare, Global Market Insights) and Black Book Research, whose figures vary widely by scope; treat any single number as directional. Vendor press releases are flagged as such.

## Which specialties have the highest and lowest EMR/EHR adoption, and which are digitizing fastest?

### Takeaway
Overall US office-based EHR adoption is now near-universal (95% any EHR, 83.6% certified, 2024), so the interesting variance is at the specialty level: cardiology/neurology/surgical specialties sit at the top (~93–96%), while behavioral health/psychiatry (~61%) and dermatology (~70%) have historically lagged most — but the widely quoted specialty gaps come from 2015 data and may have narrowed. The faster *recent* digitization story is in the previously-underserved niches (behavioral health, aesthetics, physio), where specialty software markets grow at 12–15% CAGR vs ~5% for the overall EHR market.

### Cited Findings
- **Overall US adoption 2024:** 95.0% of office-based physicians use any EHR; 83.6% use a certified EHR system — [CDC NEHRS 2024 via HealthIT.gov / search summary](https://healthit.gov/news/office-based-physician-electronic-health-record-adoption-2008-2024/)
- **Specialty rates (CAUTION — these are from the 2015 NEHRS, not 2024; the linked file is named "2015_nehrs"):** Cardiology 95.6% (highest), Neurology 94.5%, Urology 94%, General surgery 93.8%, Orthopedic surgery 93.2%, General/family practice 92.7%, Dermatology 70.2%, Behavioral health/psychiatry 61.3% (lowest) — [TechTarget: Understanding EHR Adoption With Medical Specialties](https://www.techtarget.com/searchhealthit/news/366579200/Understanding-EHR-Adoption-With-Medical-Specialties)
- **Facility-type adoption (ONC Health IT Dashboard, year unstated):** Psychiatric hospitals 49%, Long-term care hospitals 59%, Children's hospitals 87%, Rehabilitation centers 89% — [TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/searchhealthit/news/366579200/Understanding-EHR-Adoption-With-Medical-Specialties)
- **Overall EHR market growth is slow (~5% CAGR):** Global EHR market ~USD 35.89B in 2025, projected CAGR ~5.10% through 2033 (Grand View); US market ~5.30% CAGR — [search summary citing Grand View Research](https://www.marketresearch.com/Grand-View-Research-v4060/Electronic-Health-Records-44355221)
- **Previously-lagging niches are the fast movers:** Behavioral health EHR market CAGR estimated ~11.9–14.9% (Towards Healthcare USD 4.09B 2025→14.22B 2034 at 14.85%; Mordor USD 1.56B 2025→2.74B 2030 at 11.87%) — [Towards Healthcare](https://www.towardshealthcare.com/insights/behavioral-health-ehr-software-market-sizing); [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/behavioral-health-ehr-market)
- **Vendor consolidation signals a maturing replacement market:** certified EHR vendors fell from 1,200+ (2014) to ~280 (Q4 2024); ambulatory physician EHR *replacement* market estimated at $9B — [Black Book Research via Newswire](https://www.newswire.com/news/emerging-specialty-ehr-vendors-transform-clinical-workflows-with-22492186)

### Inferences
- The "adoption gap by specialty" framing is less relevant in the US than the "replacement/upgrade" opportunity: nearly everyone has *some* EHR, so growth comes from practices moving off generic or legacy systems to specialty-tailored ones. The $9B replacement figure is the demand pool.
- Behavioral health and dermatology being historically lowest-adoption + having the fastest-growing specialty software markets suggests these are "late but catching up fast" — the clearest signal of pent-up demand for tailored tooling.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm *2024* specialty-level EHR adoption percentages; the CDC 2024 weighted-estimates file groups physicians only into primary care / surgical / medical categories, and the granular specialty numbers circulating are 2015. A direct pull from the CDC NEHRS 2024 PUF would be needed for current per-specialty rates.

## Which specialties have dedicated/specialized EMR products and why do they need specialty-specific tooling?

### Takeaway
A mature ecosystem of specialty-specific EMRs exists across ~30+ specialties; ModMed is the recurring category leader in imaging/procedure-heavy fields (dermatology, ophthalmology, orthopedics, plastics, gastro, urology). The core reasons specialties need tailored tools: integrated imaging, specialty-specific templates/documentation, procedure and cash-pay billing workflows, and specialty regulatory/outcome tracking that generic EHRs handle poorly.

### Cited Findings
- **Specialties with recognized dedicated EMR segments (Black Book 2025, 17 categories named):** Addiction treatment, Behavioral health/psychiatry, Cardiology, Chiropractic, Dermatology, Gastroenterology, General practice, Home health/hospice, Neurology, OB-GYN, Oncology, Ophthalmology, Orthopedics, Pain management/anesthesiology, Pediatrics, Podiatry, Sports medicine — [Black Book via Newswire](https://www.newswire.com/news/emerging-specialty-ehr-vendors-transform-clinical-workflows-with-22492186)
- **ModMed (EMA) is the dominant specialty-EHR brand:** ranked #1 in all 11 of its specialty-specific EHRs in the 2025 Black Book survey (judged on 18 KPIs); historically #1 Dermatology EHR 10 consecutive years, #1 Ophthalmology 7 years, #1 Orthopedic 6 years — [ModMed / BusinessWire press release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250116234774/en/ModMed-Once-Again-Ranks-1-in-All-of-its-Specialty-Specific-EHRs-in-the-2025-Black-Book-Survey) (vendor-sourced)
- **ModMed appears most often across Black Book's 40-specialty list** — [Becker's Hospital Review: Top EHRs for 40 specialties](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/ehrs/top-ehr-vendor-for-40-medical-specialties.html)
- **ModMed holds ~10% of the dermatology EMR market** (product started in dermatology + ophthalmology) — [search summary]; dermatology EMR vendors also include RXNT, NueMD, NovoClinical — [Capterra Dermatology EMR](https://www.capterra.com/dermatology-emr-software/)
- **Why imaging-heavy specialties need tailored tooling:** Becker's names ophthalmology, dermatology and plastic surgery as fields where imaging outside the record creates workflow problems; integrating imaging + other functions drives financial performance for high-productivity specialty practices — [Becker's: Integration and imaging crucial to specialty practices](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/integration-and-imaging-crucial-to-specialty-practices/)
- **ModMed ophthalmology EHR natively tracks visual acuity and intraocular pressure (IOP);** behavioral health notes need PHQ-9 and GAD-7 scoring — specialty data fields generic EHRs lack — [search summaries, OmniMD / Black Book]
- **Specialty templates/dropdowns reduce data-entry errors** and align to specialty workflow — [DrChrono: Tailoring EHR to specialty needs](https://www.drchrono.com/blog/tailoring-ehr-solutions-to-meet-specialty-specific-needs/) (vendor claim)
- **Black Book's rationale:** "Dissatisfaction with generic platforms has created opportunities for agile, innovative vendors" (Doug Brown, founder). Specialty vendors win on customizable templates for multi-site practices, built-in imaging, telemedicine and diagnostic tools, plus HL7 FHIR / SMART on FHIR interoperability — [Black Book via Newswire](https://www.newswire.com/news/emerging-specialty-ehr-vendors-transform-clinical-workflows-with-22492186)
- **Migration benefit claim (unverified, single market report):** practices moving from generic to specialty EMR see 34% less documentation time and 18% better billing accuracy — [MarketIntelo Dermatology EMR report](https://marketintelo.com/report/dermatology-emr-software-market/amp) (treat with caution)

### Inferences
- The specialties with the strongest case for dedicated tooling cluster on three axes: (1) **imaging-centric** (derm, ophthalmology, plastics/aesthetics, orthopedics, radiology), (2) **structured-instrument documentation** (behavioral health PHQ-9/GAD-7, physio functional scales), and (3) **procedure/cash billing** (aesthetics, dermatology, pain management). A specialty-tailored EMR SaaS should prioritize templates in these.
- ModMed's near-total dominance of Black Book specialty rankings means a new entrant competes less on "having a specialty EMR" and more on price, local-market fit (language, billing rules), and underserved specialties ModMed doesn't cover well.

### Gaps
- No independent *installed-base market share* study by specialty (Black Book rankings are satisfaction-based, not share). "Market leader" claims rest on vendor press releases.

## What drives faster software adoption (cash-pay, imaging-heavy, high-volume, chronic-disease tracking, regulatory)?

### Takeaway
Adoption accelerates where software directly drives revenue or is clinically indispensable: cash-pay/elective specialties adopt all-in-one practice-management + payment platforms fastest because software is a growth/revenue tool (payments, memberships, marketing), while imaging-heavy and instrument-documentation specialties adopt because integrated imaging/templates are operationally essential.

### Cited Findings
- **Cash-pay practices need distinct software features:** upfront payment collection, card-on-file storage, membership management, financial reporting, text-to-pay — [Optimantra: Cash-Pay Practice Playbook](https://optimantra.com/blog/the-cash-pay-practice-playbook-ehr-setup-for-non-insurance-models) (vendor); [PatientNow press release](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/patientnow-unveils-next-generation-practice-management-capabilities-for-aesthetic-and-wellness-providers/)
- **Aesthetics/med-spa software grows fastest among niches (~14% CAGR):** medical spa management software ~USD 387–390M in 2025 → ~$1.25–1.33B by 2034 at ~13.9% CAGR; North America 39% revenue share (2025) — [Global Market Insights via GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/01/27/3015357/0/en/Medical-Spa-Management-Software-Market-to-hit-USD-1-3-billion-by-2034-says-Global-Market-Insights-Inc.html)
- **Vendor framing of cash-pay/elective momentum:** Fresha claims 140,000+ businesses in 120+ countries; Moxie positions as "modern practice management and EMR built specifically for medical aesthetics"; Meevo targets aesthetic practices that "don't need a full EMR"; Cherry adds patient financing — [search summaries, vendor sites] (self-reported)
- **Imaging integration drives financial performance** for high-productivity specialty practices — [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/integration-and-imaging-crucial-to-specialty-practices/)
- **Physical therapy EMR market ~8–10% CAGR** (USD 1.2–1.4B in 2025); broader physio software category ~USD 2.8B 2025 at ~13.4% CAGR — [Mordor Intelligence Physical Therapy Software](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/physical-therapy-software-market); [MarketIntelo Physical Therapy EMR](https://marketintelo.com/report/physical-therapy-emr-software-market)
- **Dental PMS ~9–10% CAGR** (global 2025 ~$1.5–3.2B depending on scope), driven by cloud + 3D digital tech adoption — [ResearchAndMarkets via BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250623198049/en/U.S.-Dental-Practice-Management-Software-Market-Trends-Analysis-Report-2025-2030-Digital-Technologies-Drive-Growth-with-Key-Focus-on-3D-and-Cloud-Solutions---ResearchAndMarkets.com)

### Inferences
- **Ranking of adoption drivers by strength:** (1) software-as-revenue (cash-pay/elective: aesthetics, derm cosmetic, dental) — fastest; (2) operational necessity of integrated imaging (ophthalmology, derm, ortho); (3) structured outcome tracking tied to reimbursement (behavioral health, physio); (4) regulatory mandates (strongest historically in US via Meaningful Use, now saturated). For a new specialty SaaS, the highest-willingness-to-pay segments are cash-pay/elective where software visibly grows the practice.
- The faster CAGRs of aesthetics (~14%) and behavioral health (~12–15%) vs dental/physio (~9–10%) vs overall EHR (~5%) rank-order where greenfield demand is strongest.

### Gaps
- No single independent study directly ranks adoption drivers against each other; the ranking above is inferred from relative market-growth rates and qualitative vendor/analyst statements.

## Telemedicine/digital-health by specialty; small/independent clinic adoption in emerging markets

### Takeaway
Psychiatry/behavioral health is the runaway leader in telemedicine (and its visits are fully digitizable), while dermatology and surgical specialties are low telehealth users; overall office-based telemedicine use actually declined from 2021 to 2024. In emerging markets (esp. Latin America) small/independent clinics are the laggards — blocked mainly by cost/funding — making affordable, cloud, Spanish-language software the key unlock.

### Cited Findings
- **Telemedicine by specialty — psychiatry dominates:** Doximity 2024 survey — psychiatry 66% daily telemedicine use (highest), endocrinology 48%; dermatology among the 5 lowest telehealth specialties in 2024 Medicare claims (~3.7% of eligible spending billed as telehealth) — [Doximity 2024 State of Telemedicine](https://www.doximity.com/reports/state-of-telemedicine-report/2024); [AMA telehealth-by-specialty analysis](https://www.ama-assn.org/practice-management/digital-health/new-data-details-how-telehealth-use-varies-physician-specialty)
- **Pandemic-era spread (2020 integrated-system study):** telemedicine ranged from 3.2% of visits (dermatology) to 98.3% (psychiatry) — [PMC study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11058596)
- **Overall office-based telemedicine use DECLINED 2021→2024:** all physicians 86.5%→80.0%; primary care 91.4%→86.2%; surgical 74.8%→60.1% (significant decline); medical 87.2%→83.2% (no significant change) — [CDC NCHS, Telemedicine Use Among Office-based Physicians 2021 and 2024](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/data/hestat/hestat120.htm)
- **Emerging-market small clinics lag, cost is the barrier:** 2025 Mexico report — smaller hospitals/community clinics hardest hit by cost/technical hurdles; growth concentrated in urban/academic systems. Vendors for small practices: SISNova, ClinicCloud (Spanish-language cloud EHR), Softmedic (cost-effective for smaller providers) — [Black Book Mexico EHR report via Newswire](https://www.newswire.com/news/2025-mexico-ehr-market-report-technology-growth-amid-structural-and-22546284)
- **Brazil funding barrier:** 96% of Brazilian healthcare providers cited insufficient funding as the primary obstacle, disproportionately hitting smaller/rural clinics; Brazil is LatAm's most active EHR market (led by Philips, MV) but large regional procurements fell ~20% in 2024 vs 2023 — [Black Book Brazil report via Newswire](https://www.newswire.com/news/brazils-digital-healthcare-boom-faces-critical-hurdles-reveals-black-22558272)
- **Mexico private-sector lag (undated, unverified):** Mexico private adoption described as "stagnant," lagging Brazil and Chile which reportedly exceed 50% adoption — [Mexico Business News](https://mexicobusiness.news/health/news/ehrs-digital-foundation-patient-centric-care-mexico) (could not verify the 50% figure)
- **LatAm interoperability momentum:** region accelerating health-data interoperability via openEHR — [Newswire](https://www.newswire.com/news/latin-america-accelerates-health-data-interoperability-with-openehr-a-22631341)

### Inferences
- Telemedicine fit maps onto the same specialties as EMR greenfield demand: behavioral health is both lowest historical EMR adoption AND highest telemedicine use — a strong combined signal to build behavioral-health templates + integrated telehealth early.
- In emerging markets the differentiator is not features but **affordability + cloud + local language/billing**. For an independent-doctor/small-clinic SaaS in LatAm, price and Spanish-language cloud delivery beat ModMed-style feature depth. The named incumbents (ClinicCloud, SISNova, Softmedic) define the competitive floor.
- The 2021→2024 telemedicine *decline* means telehealth should be a feature, not the core wedge, except in behavioral health/psychiatry where it's structurally sticky.

### Gaps
- No rigorous 2024–2025 dataset on small-clinic EMR *penetration* specifically in LatAm (KLAS LatAm report and IDB guidance are both 2020). Country-level private-practice adoption rates (Mexico, Colombia, Peru) remain unquantified from reliable primary sources.
- Doximity figures are single-platform self-selected users; not representative of all physicians. CDC and Doximity measure different things (share of physicians vs daily use among platform users) and should not be compared directly.
